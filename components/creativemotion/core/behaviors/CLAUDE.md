# core/behaviors — balance (M5b), stepping (M5c) and hit reaction (M5d)

Depends on `core/math`, `core/rig`, `core/motor`, `core/sensors`, `core/arbiter` and `physics/interface`. It uses `kinematic/ik` for stepping (allowed by toddhow, 2026-10-19; task 0019). Never Jolt. Specs: `docs/tasks/0017` (balance, hit reaction, the arbiter) and `docs/tasks/0019` (stepping).

## Rules
- `Balance` is data from the rig and profile only: leg joints, ankle bones (the profile's feet) and hip bones (each leg's joint bone nearest the root) are found by walking from each foot body to the root body. No bone names in code.
- `Balance::apply` leans the target pose, never the bodies: ankle `R(u, −θa)`, hip `R(u, +θh)` (sign reversed by the 2026-10-20 amendment), `u = normalize(e × z)`, `e` from the mean of the support vertices to the capture point. No reading, no support or no offset leaves the pose unchanged. Allocates nothing.
- `BalanceDriver` replaces `motor::RagdollDriver` for a ragdoll with balance: first step sets the leg motors, then every pre-step pose → sensor → lean → `driveTowardsPose`. It keeps a pointer to the ragdoll (the host keeps it alive and removes the driver first) and allocates nothing per step (`opas_alloc_tests`).
- Gains live in the profile's `balance` object; the sample values are tuned against the stand and push tests at 30 Hz × 4, 60 Hz × 2 and 120 Hz × 1 sub-steps.
- `Stepping` (M5c, replaced by task 0019, `docs/tasks/0019`) follows Coros, Beaudoin and van de Panne 2010: the swing foot goes to the inverted pendulum point (`ipmStepOffset`), placed by two-bone IK on both legs (`kinematic/ik`, knee pole and limits from the rig), and a virtual force on the centre of mass brakes the body through stance-leg and trunk joint torques (`virtualForceTorque`), with gravity compensation on the swing leg. The three pure helpers (`ipmStepOffset`, `virtualForceTorque`, `jointCoupleImpulses`) hold the formulas so they can be tested without physics.
- `BalanceDriver` with stepping runs Leaning → Stepping → Settling (→ Stepping again) or GaveUp. Stepping arms only after the capture point has been inside for `armTimeS`, because landing from a drop reads as outside. Settling continues the sequence at once if the capture point slips back out before re-arming. GaveUp is final.
- Every torque stepping adds is a joint couple: +tau on the joint's head-side body and -tau on the other, each as two opposite impulses 0.1 m either side of the body centre (`jointCoupleImpulses`), capped at `velocityTuning.maxTorqueNm`. Nothing pushes the body from outside (the M5c trunk assist, a couple on a single body, is gone). `lastJointTorqueNm()` reports the largest applied this step. Distal body sets and masses are precomputed in `Stepping::create`, so nothing allocates per step.
- The swing foot is the one nearer the capture point (M5c's rule; the farther one crosses over after a sideways push), and `stanceHalfWidthM` only stops the feet crossing: the swing foot lands at least twice that to its own side of the stance foot across the body.

- `HitReaction` (M5d) precomputes body-graph distances (one edge per joint) and keeps each joint's drop and ramp start; a joint's distance is the smaller of its two bodies' distances. `BalanceDriver` times a reported hit at the start of the next pre-step and sets a joint's motor only when its value (base gain × multiplier) changes: the leg motors on the first step, every recovering joint each step after a hit, nothing on quiet steps.
- With an arbiter, `BalanceDriver` lets go when the state becomes Falling: no lean, stepping or joint torques; over `blendTimeS` (counted by the driver from the moment it lets go, not restarted by Falling → Fallen) the leg gains move linearly from the balance leg motor to the profile's and the targets slerp from the last balance pose to the source pose. `Balance::profileMotor` gives each joint's profile gains.

## Pitfalls
- Balance needs Jolt's solver at >= 120 Hz internally (`WorldConfig::subStepsPerStep`); at 60 Hz with one sub-step the planted legs do not converge and the ragdoll falls.
- The tuning margin is thin at 30 Hz: standing feet drift 9 mm against the 1 cm limit. Retune with the stand and push tests at all three rates after changing the rig, the leg motor or the gains.
- World axes are taken as model axes: a scenario root yaw is ignored by the lean (an M5 limit in the spec).
- The lean axis `u` is a world axis applied to a bone's *local* rotation, so it acts in the parent bone's frame. That is right only while bone rest frames are unrotated, as in the sample rig; a rig with rotated bone frames (imported skeletons, later milestones) needs the axis taken into each parent's frame first (an M5 limit, found by the M5b review).
- Each bone leans at most once: ankle and hip bones are de-duplicated, so legs that meet below the root body share one hip lean, and a bone that is an ankle is never also a hip.
- Pelvis tilt must be measured against the body's rest rotation: the sample pelvis capsule lies across the hips, so its local +Z is horizontal when upright.
- Stepping is chaotic: a numerically trivial change (floor size, world capacity) changes Jolt's solve order and can flip a run between standing and falling. Tune with the robustness search (task 0019 used 24 setups across floor sizes, capacities and the three rates, and pushes in four directions), never against one setup. Good values sit on narrow ridges; their neighbours lose several setups.
- Stepping needs exactly two feet, each a three-joint leg (hip, knee, ankle) whose hip hangs from the root body's bone. The knee bends about its joint's twist axis, towards the larger side of the twist range.
- A fall during a step or while settling is not reported as GaveUp; M5d's Falling state covers it.
- Forward pushes recover with a forward step that brakes (task 0019). Backward and sideways pushes are an accepted limit (task 0019, "Accepted limit"): backward steps do not go backwards, and sideways pushes end standing less often than with M5c (with the sample: +X 12 against 17, -X 17 against 19 of 24 setups; accepted by toddhow on 2026-10-22). Forward pushes want gain 1000, backward ones about 700 with shorter steps; one setting does not serve all directions.
- The virtual braking force needs about ten times Coros' gain (1000 against 100 N s/m) because a standing push must be absorbed in a step or two, not corrected over a walk (`docs/research/0001`).
- The legs are set by IK in model space with the pelvis target treated as upright and unrotated: a ragdoll with a yaw steps as if facing model +Y (an M5 limit, kept by task 0019). `legsByIk` also overwrites the hip, knee and ankle local rotations with model-space rotations, so it assumes bone rest frames are unrotated (true of the sample rig); an imported skeleton needs them taken into each bone's frame first.
- The hip target is built for a hinge knee: shortest arc for the thigh, then a twist that keeps the knee axis on the bend plane's normal; the knee is a pure rotation about its own axis. Setting the hip to the IK's shortest-arc rotation alone twisted the knee axis out of the plane whenever a foot was to the side, so the knee motor fought its hinge (task 0019 review).
