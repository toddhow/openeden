## 2. What the GTAO pass produces today and why it is noisy

`files/shaders/post/ao.frag` (and `ao.slang`) estimate the visibility of a half-resolution pixel with `ao slices` (2) slice directions and `ao steps` (6) steps on each side of each slice, which is 24 depth reads. The slice direction of a pixel is `(slice + noise.x) / slices * pi` and the position of its steps along the slice is `(k - noise.y) / steps`, where `noise` comes from interleaved gradient noise of the pixel position (`ao.slang`, `gradientNoise`). **The noise has no time term.** The result is a fixed noise pattern: the picture does not flicker, and a still scene has no temporal variance at all, whatever it is. The tonemap's 5x5 depth-and-normal-aware filter (`aoFiltered`, `Render::Ao::bilateralWeight`) turns that pattern into a smooth but biased value (it averages the 25 pixels' different direction samples, which is as good as 50 slices but at 5x5 pixels' blur), and its edges follow the half-resolution grid.

So the gain from accumulation is of two kinds, which the measurement must separate:

1. **Less error** against the true integral: each frame samples other directions and offsets, so the average over N frames approaches a 2N-slice, 6N-step result at each pixel, and the spatial filter no longer has to hide all the noise (it can be narrower, or the bias it brings stays the same while the variance goes).
2. **No gain in a naive "temporal variance" measure**, if the noise is not moved: the off path's temporal variance is zero. The honest comparison is therefore three configurations of the same still scene: (a) the shipped pass, (b) the same pass with the noise moved every frame and no history, which has the highest temporal variance of the three, and (c) accumulation. (c) must have a smaller temporal variance than (b), and a smaller error than (a) against a reference with many more samples.

## 3. Moving the noise each frame

- Jimenez et al. (**recalled** from the snippet) rotate over 6 frames with a 4x4 spatial pattern. XeGTAO moves a spatially-fixed R2 pattern by an offset per frame, on a 64-frame cycle.
- This work keeps the existing spatial pattern (interleaved gradient noise) and adds a per-frame offset, a two-dimensional additive recurrence (Roberts' R2): `offset(n) = frac(0.5 + n * (0.7548776662466927, 0.5698402909980532))`, `n = frame mod 64`, added to the pixel's noise and wrapped (`frac(noise + offset)`). Direction and step offsets are the two components. R2 is low-discrepancy in the plane, so within any few consecutive frames the (direction, step) pairs are well spread, which is what makes the average converge faster than independent random numbers. A cycle of 64 is long enough that the effective history (about 10 frames at weight 0.9) never sees the seam. The frame index is a counter advanced in `RenderManager::onFrame`, never a clock (project rule for determinism).
- The additive offset of a value in [0, 1) followed by a wrap keeps the spatial pattern's good distribution across the screen and shifts it uniformly; with offset 0 the noise is exactly the shipped one.

## 4. Reprojection and history rejection

- **Where the old value was.** The velocity image (`Render::Velocity`, `currentUV - previousUV`, RG16F, full resolution) gives, for the pixel's surface, where it was in the previous frame: `previousUV = uv - velocity` (camera motion from depth and the two matrices, plus skinned actors' bone motion; rigid objects that move as a whole, a door or a thrown object, have camera motion only, task 0026 stage c). The AO image is half resolution: the pixel's position, depth and normal are those of the full-resolution texel the pass samples, and the velocity is read from the same texel.
- **TAA's jitter.** When TAA is on, the G-buffer images are drawn with a sub-pixel shift and the velocity assumes the unjittered camera. A pixel at `uv` shows the surface point that an unjittered camera sees at `uv - jitterUV` and the history texel that point lives in was recorded at `previousUV + previousJitterUV`: the history is read at `uv - velocity + (previousJitterUV - jitterUV)`. With TAA off both are zero. This uses `RenderManager::temporalState()`.
- **Four taps, each judged.** The history is read at the four texels around the reprojected position (bilinear weights computed by hand, no hardware filtering across a depth edge). Each tap carries its own depth and normal (stored with the history, because the G-buffer is overwritten every frame), and is given weight `bilinear * (1 - depthMismatch) * normalMatch`. A tap that is another surface contributes nothing; this is the way SVGF (**recalled**) rejects history. Depth: the current surface's depth expressed in the previous camera (`w` of the clip position of `toPreviousClip * (viewPosition, 1)`, the way TAA's disocclusion test does) against the stored depth, compared as a difference of `log2(1 + d)`. Normal: the current view-space normal rotated into the previous view space (the rotation from the two view matrices) against the stored normal, by their dot product.
- **Why the normal test is the one that catches a door.** A rigid object that moves has no object velocity, so its history is fetched from where the camera says it was. At the door's leading edge the history holds what was behind it: a different depth, rejected by depth. In the middle of the door, if the door swings so that its depth at a pixel is unchanged, only its normal has changed. A wall pixel beside a door that opens is another matter: its own depth and normal did not change but its occlusion did. That is not ghosting but a lag of the accumulated value, and the only protections are the history weight cap and a neighbourhood clamp (section 5).
- **Counting.** Each pixel keeps `n`, the number of frames behind its value. The new value is `lerp(current, history, w)` with `w = confidence * min(base, n / (n + 1))` (the SVGF form, **recalled**): the first accumulated frame averages two samples, and the weight settles at `base` (0.9) after nine. `confidence` is the sum of the four taps' weights (0 to 1), and the new count is `1 + n * confidence` capped, so a partly rejected pixel starts to forget. A reset (first frame, resize, camera cut, gap) sets the weight to 0: the pixel takes the new sample and starts at 1.
- **Format.** An 8-bit accumulation buffer cannot hold an exponential average at 0.9: the step `(1 - w) * (current - history)` for a small difference rounds to zero. The history and the resolved image are 32-bit float (four channels: the value, the encoded depth, the count, the packed normal), so that nothing but the two samples decides the value.

## 5. Where to put the blur relative to the accumulation

Two orders are used in games: spatial filter first (the raw samples are blurred, then accumulated) and temporal first (accumulated raw samples, blurred when read). XeGTAO's README (**read**) puts the spatial 5x5 in the AO pass and leaves the temporal to TAA; Jimenez et al. (**recalled**) accumulate first and reconstruct spatially after (the order given in the snippet: reprojection and accumulation, then the 4x4 bilateral). This renderer's tonemap already filters when it reads the AO (the 5x5, `Render::Ao::bilateralWeight`), so **accumulation happens first and the existing filter stays the last step**: the accumulated image is unblurred, the history holds raw samples (it is not blurred again and again), and the tonemap's code does not change at all. The cost is that the filter's blur is still there; with the noise averaged away it could be narrower, which is a later change and not part of this task.

**The neighbourhood clamp.** The history is limited to the range of the current frame's samples around the pixel, to bound the lag of a changed occlusion. A single AO sample with 2 slices is very noisy (its 3x3 neighbourhood in a smooth area spans most of [0, 1]), so a min/max box is nearly useless and a tight box would throw away the converged value and keep the noise. The clamp is therefore a **variance range** (the mean of the 3x3 neighbourhood plus or minus gamma standard deviations, restricted to the pixels that are the same surface by depth and normal), with gamma a setting (`ao temporal clamp`, 0 switches it off), and whether it helps is **measured** (the noise with and without it, and the lag of a changed occluder), not assumed. The variance box is Salvi's method, the subject of the patent noted in 0037 section 9; the claims were not read. It is behind its own setting, off at 0, so dropping it is one default. **toddhow decides** (task 0076, decisions).

## 6. Contact shadows

**What they are.** A short screen-space ray from each pixel toward the sun (typically some tens of centimetres to a metre, 8 to 16 steps along the depth buffer) that finds small occluders the shadow maps cannot resolve: the shadow where a rock meets the ground, a finger on a cup. They darken the **direct** light only.

**The decision: left out of this task, with these reasons.**

1. **The renderer stores no separate direct-light term.** What the G-buffer separates is the ambient part (`GBuffer::ambientFormat`, the fifth extra output); the colour is the sum of everything. Darkening `colour - ambient` would also darken specular highlights, emissive surfaces (glowing mushrooms, lamps), point lights and the fog's share, and in interiors, where there is no dominant directional light, the scene's light direction (`mLightPosition`) is a placeholder. Darkening the ambient output, as ambient occlusion does, would be the wrong light. A correct implementation needs a **sun-diffuse output** next to the ambient one (another extra colour output of `HdrTarget`, written by `default/main.frag` and `terrain/main.frag`, with the cost in bandwidth and a change to two large shaders that the Slang port, task 0072, is still working on).
2. **The sun's direction and the depth are available to a pass, but the effect then needs three more things:** the tonemap's AO variant to read a second channel (both its GLSL and its Slang twin, whose SPIR-V `PostPassesSwitchOff` compares with the old strings), the AO pass to write it (same constraint), and a fade by the sun's elevation and the weather (clouds, storms, night). That is a second feature, not a companion.
3. **The shadow cascades already exist** (`[Shadows] enable shadows`, task 0052), with their own resolution limits; contact shadows are the cure for exactly the finest of those limits and are best judged with them on.

**What a later task would do:** add a sun-diffuse output (R11G11B10 or RGBA16F), a contact-shadow pass (a Slang compute pass sharing the history machinery built here), and apply `lerp(1, visibility, strength * sunFade)` to the output in the tonemap. This is recorded as a decision for toddhow, not built.

## 7. How it fits this renderer

- **Cost.** The GTAO pass costs about 0.28 ms at 720p (task 0029); accumulation adds a 3x3 read, four history reads and one write per half-resolution pixel. It replaces the fragment AO pass when it is on (the generation is repeated in the compute shader, because the existing shader files cannot change without breaking `PostPassesSwitchOff`, which compares their SPIR-V with the strings they came from, and a fragment stage cannot be a Slang twin beside a GLSL vertex stage: "a mixed pipeline draws nothing", `files/shaders/CLAUDE.md`).
- **TAA.** With `taa` on, the frame index comes from `temporalState()` (the two stay in step) and so does the jitter. With it off, a counter of its own, and the jitter is zero. The resets are the same function, `Render::Taa::historyReset`, fed with the same camera data in `RenderManager::updateVelocity`.
- **The ambient image is not accumulated.** The tonemap subtracts `ambient * (1 - ao)` where `ambient` is the current frame's (jittered, with TAA) ambient output and the colour is the resolved picture; at a silhouette they disagree by a sub-pixel. That limit predates this work (task 0075, stage log) and is not changed.
