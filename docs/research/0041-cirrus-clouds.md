## 2. What cirrus is, and what the eye notices

- **Where and what.** Cirrus, cirrocumulus and cirrostratus are the high-level cloud genera, with bases at about 5 to 13 km (temperate latitudes 5 to 12 km; Meteoblue puts cirrus proper at 8 to 12 km), made almost exclusively of ice crystals (WMO atlas). Low ice water content makes them nearly transparent: in the classification of Sassen and Cho (**recalled**) a thin cirrus has a visible optical depth between about 0.03 and 0.3, and only above 0.3 does it dim the sun noticeably (a transmittance `exp(-0.3)` is 0.74).
- **Shape.** Cirrus is fibrous: ice crystals large enough to fall form **fall streaks** ("mares' tails") that wind shear bends into hooks and curls; cirrostratus is a thin uniform veil; cirrocumulus is a field of small ripples. All three are thin sheets, one or two kilometres thick at most, so for a ground observer the shape is a **2D pattern** on a distant surface. The parallax of a thick volume is not there; this is why games draw it flat.
- **Wind.** The air at cirrus height moves fast (jet-stream winds of 30 m/s and more; **recalled**). In angle on the sky this is slower than it sounds, because the cloud is far: a pattern directly overhead at height `h` moves with angular speed `v / h`. **Derived:** 30 m/s at 9 km is 0.0033 rad/s, 0.19 degrees a second, 11 degrees a minute; the repository's volumetric layer at 5 m/s (`windMetersPerSecondPerSpeed` 4 times the clear weather's cloud speed 1.25, `cloudvolumemath.hpp`) at its middle height of 2.75 km is 0.0018 rad/s, 0.10 degrees a second. So a physically proportioned cirrus layer (6 times the speed, 3.3 times the height) drifts about twice as fast across the sky as the low layer, not six times. The wind also tends to **turn with height** (it veers, clockwise in the northern hemisphere, **recalled**), so the cirrus drifts in a different direction from the low layer; the project's one wind direction (`windDirection`, 0.958, 0.287) is a fixed vector, and a rotation of 20 to 40 degrees for the high layer costs nothing.
- **The sky distance.** A thin layer at altitude `h` over a spherical Earth of radius `R` is met by a ray from the ground with direction cosine `dz` (up) at distance `t = -R dz + sqrt(R^2 dz^2 + 2 R h + h^2)` (**derived** by intersecting the ray with the sphere of radius `R + h`). Overhead `t = h`; at the horizon `t = sqrt(2 R h + h^2)`, **338 km** for `h` = 9 km and `R` = 6371 km. So the layer fills the sky down to the horizon and gets very stretched there; a flat-plane mapping `t = h / dz` goes to infinity and must be clamped or faded. This is a visible property of real cirrus (bands converging to the horizon).
- **How much detail a pixel can show.** At the horizon a pixel (about 0.055 degrees, 9.6e-4 rad, at 1080p and a 60 degree vertical field of view) covers `t^2 * alpha / h` along the view direction (**derived** for a flat layer seen at a grazing angle): 12 km at 338 km, 1 km only above about 5 degrees of elevation. A fibre field with 0.5 to 2 km features is therefore **sub-pixel below roughly 5 degrees**, and the shader must fade its fine octaves by that footprint or it will shimmer; the layer should blend into a smooth band near the horizon, which is also what the eye sees (aerial perspective).
- **Light.** Cirrus scatters light strongly **forward**, mostly diffraction and refraction by ice (asymmetry parameter about 0.8; Fu 1996, **recalled**), which brightens it near the sun (and, for cirrostratus, produces the 22 degree halo; the halo is left out of the recommendation). Its **colour** at sunrise and sunset: it is so high that it stays sunlit after the ground is dark, and the light that reaches it has crossed a long slant path through the lower air, so it glows pink or orange against a darkening sky. **Derived:** a layer directly overhead at height `h` stops being lit when the sun is `acos(R / (R + h))` below the horizon: 2.5 degrees for 6 km, **3.0 degrees for 9 km**, 3.5 degrees for 12 km (first-order geometry; the layer toward the sun stays lit longer, away from it shorter). A cumulus layer at 3 km is lit until 1.8 degrees. The atmosphere model's own bounds are `nightEdge` = -4 degrees and `dayEdge` = +6 degrees (`atmospheremath.hpp dayWeight`), so cirrus lighting fits inside the model's twilight window.
- **Shadow.** A thin cirrus attenuates the sun by 3 to 25 percent, and its shadow is spread by the sun's angular diameter (0.0093 rad) over its height: a penumbra of **about 84 m at 9 km (derived)**, which blurs a fibre pattern of a few hundred metres to nothing. Cirrus does not shadow the ground in any way a viewer sees. The repository's cloud shadow grids and the volumetric layer's ground shadow (`cloudgpu.hpp`, binding 16) should not include it.
- **Weather.** Cirrus is the cloud of fair weather and of the leading edge of fronts; it is invisible under overcast low clouds (the low cloud is in front of it) and above storms we cannot see it. It cannot be observed in fog or in snowfall. Morrowind's weather records have no cirrus field.

## 3. What the repository has today

- **Two textured layers.** `Sky` (`apps/openmw/mwrender/weather.cpp`) holds two `Clouds` domes (`mClouds`, `mNextClouds`): a NIF model (`meshes/sky_clouds_01.nif`, a flat dome of five rings, `Render::Cloud::domeRadius` and `domeHeight`) drawn with the pipeline `sky/clouds` (`Clouds::pipelineOptions`: `depthOff`, blend, `DIFFUSE_MAP`, `TEXMAT`, `MATERIAL`), the weather's picture scrolled by a texture matrix (`mCloudAnimationTimer += duration * mCloudSpeed * 0.003`), a tint (`mCloudTint`) and an alpha for the blend between two weathers (`mCloudBlendFactor`). Both are children of `mSwitch`, after the sun and the two moons.
- **The sky shader** `files/shaders/sky/clouds/main.frag` (and its Slang twin `clouds.frag.slang`): the picture times `(fog colour + 0.13) * material.diffuse`, optionally the lit look (`lit.glsl`: transmittance, forward phase g = 0.7, silver lining, underside shade, scaled by the weather share in `material.specular.a`) and the volumetric layer's picture when `material.ambient.a > 1.5`. The vertex shader gives `domeDir` (the vertex in dome space) and `sunLocal` (the sun direction in dome space).
- **An optional ray-marched layer** at 1500 to 4000 m (task 0042): coverage from the cloud picture's mean thickness through `coverageFromThickness` (clear 0.19 gives 0.39, cloudy 0.43 gives 0.63, overcast 0.96 gives 1), wind from `windStep` and `windDirection`, a weather share from `volumeShareTarget(isStorm, landFogDepth)` that is 0 for storms and for fog depth above 0.85 to 0.95, eased over 3 seconds. Its decision D2 already said: "a second thin high layer (cirrus) as a flat textured sheet, the existing cloud texture" (`docs/tasks/0042-rendering-volumetric-clouds.md`), and `components/render/CLAUDE.md` lists "a second (cirrus) layer" under "Not built".
- **Noise with a C++ twin and regression values:** `Render::CloudVolume::hashUint`, `hashCell`, `perlin(x, y, z, period, seed)` and `perlinFbm` (`cloudvolumemath.hpp`), which the compute shader repeats bit for bit and `testcloudvolumemath.cpp` pins.
- **The atmosphere** (task 0034) is a CPU model run once a frame in `WeatherManager::applyAtmosphere(result, sunHeight)` (`apps/openmw/mwworld/weather.cpp`), where `sunHeight` is `Render::Sun::tableElevation(hour, windows)` when `[Rendering] sun follows colour tables` is on (default) and the drawn sun's elevation otherwise. It changes colours only. The shader never sees the elevation; it sees `scene.lightViewPos` and `scene.lightDiffuse`.
- **At night the scene light is the manager's mirrored moon light, with positive z** (`atmospheremath.hpp sunElevation(sunPosition, night)`, which negates the angle at night). A cirrus shader that took the sun's elevation from `scene.lightViewPos` would treat the moon as a sun. The elevation or the day weight has to travel in the material, the way the cloud share already does (`Material::setCloudShare`, `specular.a`).
- **The sun glow** (task 0040) is added to the fog colour and the atmosphere dome, not to the cloud layers (`components/render/CLAUDE.md`: "glow in the cloud layer" is under "Not built"). The sun disc is drawn before the cloud layers with depth off.
- **A precedent for a new, Slang-only sky layer:** the procedural sun (`Sky::Sky`: `Graphics::hasSlangShader("sky/sunproc")`, `slangOnlyPaths`, a `sky/sunproc/main.vert.spv`/`main.frag.spv` pair, task 0047).

## 4. How games draw them

1. **A textured layer with a flowmap or a uniform distortion** (Unity HDRP Cloud Layer, **read**): cheap, art directable, tiled; needs a painted or generated map; motion by distortion or flowmap; self-shadowing by a short 2D march. In this project it needs a texture we author or generate, since Bethesda's pictures are not ours to ship (a generated map costs memory and shows tiling).
2. **A procedural, noise-based layer on a dome** (common in games and demos; **recalled**): the fragment shader computes a density from fractal noise of a coordinate on the layer plane, with domain warping and an anisotropic stretch for fibres, and a time term for the evolution. No texture, infinite variety, resolution independent; cost is ALU per sky pixel. This is the recommendation.
3. **A second ray-marched slab** (Schneider-style, **recalled**): physically richest, twice the cost of the first layer for a pattern whose depth no one can perceive from the ground; **not recommended**.
4. **Analytic terms in the sky dome shader**: adding the cirrus inside `sky/atmosphere`. Saves one draw but couples the cirrus to a pinned, twinned GLSL family (`PostPassesSwitchOff`, `SlangPermutations`) and breaks "off is byte-identical"; **not recommended**.

## 5. The recommended model (a procedural layer on its own dome)

- **Geometry.** A third dome, a hemisphere of about 32 by 8 quads that reaches slightly below the horizon, built in code, drawn by a Slang-only pair `sky/cirrus` (registered on `Graphics::slangOnlyPaths` like `sky/sunproc`), after the moons and before `mClouds` and `mNextClouds`. Behind the low layers (they overlap it), in front of the sun disc (the veil dims the sun a little), with depth off. The vertex shader gives the view direction; the fragment shader does everything else. (Not the vanilla dome's NIF: it is a flat disc whose rim is 5.7 degrees above the horizon, and its rows fade through vertex alpha.)
- **Position on the layer.** The ray's distance to the shell `t` (section 2) and the layer coordinate `p = t * (dx, dy)` in metres, plus the accumulated wind drift. Fibre axes: rotate `p` into the wind frame (the cirrus wind direction, the low layer's `windDirection` rotated by a veer angle) and stretch the across-wind coordinate (fibres 4 to 8 times longer than wide).
- **Density.** `d = remap(fbm(warp(p), time), 1 - coverage, 1)` with 3 to 5 octaves of `Render::CloudVolume::perlin` (period 128 cells so the pattern does not repeat within the view), a domain warp from one or two lower octaves of the same noise (shear-like: the warp displacement is largest across the wind), a fall-streak term that elongates the pattern along a second noise's direction, and a smoothstep to a thin range of optical depth: `tau = tauMax * d`, `tauMax` about 0.3 (**recalled** class limit), `alpha = 1 - exp(-tau)`. Each octave is faded by the pixel's footprint on the layer (section 2) so the horizon does not shimmer.
- **Time.** The field drifts with the accumulated wind (summed in double in `Sky::update` as the volumetric wind is, wrapped by the noise period, so nothing depends on a clock) and evolves slowly by a third noise coordinate advancing with game time (a morph over minutes, not seconds).
- **Light and colour.** `colour = layerBase * (ambient + sun)`. `layerBase` follows the existing cloud layers' tint (`fog colour + 0.13`, times `material.diffuse`) so the cirrus does not look foreign beside them; `sun` is the sun's colour (`atmospheremath.hpp sunGlowTint(elevation)`, the model's transmittance normalised) times a forward phase (`henyeyGreenstein`, g 0.7 to 0.8, relative to an even spread, capped) times a **lit weight** from the true sun elevation `e`: 1 above the horizon and `1 - smoothstep(0, depressionLimit(h), -e)` below it, `depressionLimit(h) = acos(R / (R + h))`. At night the weight is 0 and the cirrus is its ambient term only (nearly invisible, as real cirrus is on a moonless night; moonlit cirrus is left out). The elevation and weight are computed on the CPU (`WeatherManager::applyAtmosphere` knows the elevation of the colour tables; the drawn sun's `sunElevation` the other) and carried in the material.
- **Weather.** A share target `s` derived from values that exist: `s = (1 - smoothstep(0.55, 0.95, lowCoverage)) * volumeShareTarget(isStorm, fogDepth)`, with `lowCoverage = blendCoverage(coverageFromThickness(current picture), coverageFromThickness(next picture), blendFactor)`, eased over 3 seconds (`easeShare`). With the numbers of the table above, clear weather (low coverage 0.39) gives 1, cloudy (0.63) about 0.9, overcast (1) 0; storms, fog and snow 0. Rain and thunderstorm pictures are thick, so they come out near 0 without special cases (**verify** each vanilla weather's mean thickness in a frame check). A slowly varying "synoptic" factor from the game date (a hash of the day number, 0.4 to 1) gives some days more cirrus than others and costs nothing; optional.
- **Atmosphere and sun glow.** The cirrus colour follows the elevation the atmosphere model uses (section 3), so the sky and the cirrus agree at dusk. The glow task 0040 adds is in the fog and the sky dome; the cirrus's forward lobe brightens it near the sun too, and the two must not add up to a second sun: the cirrus phase is capped (acceptance test in the spec).
- **Shadows.** None: the cirrus never enters `shadow()`, the cloud shadow header or the volumetric shadow map (section 2 explains why this is physical).
- **Cost.** Per sky pixel, 3 to 5 octaves of 3D Perlin for the shape, 1 to 2 for the warp and a few dozen arithmetic operations: of the order of 10 to 20 Perlin evaluations at the high quality (**estimate**; the existing lit clouds are "a few tens of operations" and measured as lost in the noise, `docs/research/0019-cloud-cost.md`). The dome is drawn with depth off, so pixels later covered by terrain are shaded too, as the other layers are; a branch on `dz` below the layer's horizon cut-off and on the share being 0 returns at once. On the RTX 4070 (GPU main scene 0.14 to 0.35 ms) this is expected below 0.1 ms at 1080p; **to be measured** in stage c. CPU cost: one small uniform update a frame.
- **Not modelled:** the 22 degree halo and sun dogs, moonlit cirrus, true cirrus from the weather transition (cirrus thickening before rain), cirrus seen in water reflections (the reflection pass draws the vanilla layers only; **verify**), cirrus seen from above (a camera over 9 km is a levitation corner case), lightning light.
