# Task: Rendering, a procedural sun (and moon) driven by the atmosphere model
Design doc section: `docs/research/0008-modern-rendering-proposal.md`, section 5 (R6); builds on `docs/tasks/0034-rendering-weather-atmosphere.md` (the model, the sun colour, "Finding for toddhow (decision 4)"), `0025-rendering-f2-linear-hdr.md` (HDR and bloom), `0023-rendering-groundwork-f0-f1.md` (the Slang pipeline, whose proof was the sun shader) and `docs/decisions/0009-*` (new shaders in Slang); related: `0040-rendering-precipitation-lightning-sun-glow.md` (the glow in fog) and `0042-rendering-volumetric-clouds.md` (clouds in front of the sun). `docs/decisions/0010-rendering-and-studio-answers-2026-11-01.md`, answer 4.
Goal: with a new setting, the sun is drawn as a computed disc (limb darkening, a soft edge, a corona and glow that fall off smoothly, an HDR intensity that bloom can use) instead of the texture `tx_sun_05.dds`, its colour and size follow the atmosphere model, and sunrise and sunset happen at the hours of Morrowind's colour tables, not at the hour the drawn sun touches the horizon; the moons get the same treatment if it costs little. Off by default and frame-identical when off.
Inherited code touched (to be confirmed at the commit that starts each stage): `apps/openmw/mwrender/weather.cpp` (`class CelestialBody`, `class Sun`, `class SunGlare`, `class Moon`: the sun's pipeline options `sky/sun` with `MATERIAL` and `DIFFUSE_MAP`, the flash `tx_sun_flash_grey_05.dds`, the occlusion query that gives `visibleRatio`), `apps/openmw/mwrender/weather.hpp` (`Sky`), `apps/openmw/mwworld/weather.cpp` (`WeatherManager::update`: the sun direction block, `sunHeight`, `glareFade`, `applyAtmosphere`; `TimeOfDayInterpolator::getValue`, whose sunrise and sunset windows come from `mTimeSettings`), `files/shaders/sky/sun/main.frag`, `main.vert`, `sun.slang`, `files/shaders/sky/sunglare/*`, `files/shaders/sky/moon/*`, `files/shaders/CMakeLists.txt`, `components/render/atmospheremath.hpp`, `components/render/tonemap.*` and `hdrtarget.*` (read: how bloom takes its input), `components/settings/categories/rendering.hpp`, `files/settings-default.cfg`, `apps/components_tests/CMakeLists.txt`.

## What is there now (read from the code)

`Sun` (`apps/openmw/mwrender/weather.cpp`) is a camera-following quad (`CelestialBody`: scale 450, placed 1000 units away along the sun direction by `setDirection`) drawn in the sky bin with depth off and blending on, using the pipeline `sky/sun` with the texture `tx_sun_05.dds` and a material whose emissive colour is the weather's sun colour (`setColor`). On top of it, in `Bin_SunGlare`, an occlusion query counts how many samples of the sun are visible (`visibleRatio`) and drives a "flash" quad (`tx_sun_flash_grey_05.dds`, scaled up to 2.6 times) and the `SunGlare` overlay quad (colour `Weather_Sun_Glare_Fader_Color`, alpha from `glareView * visibleRatio * glareTimeOfDayFade`). The fragment shader (`sky/sun/main.frag`) outputs `texture(diffuseMap, uv) * emissive`, with alpha times `scene.skyColor.a`, and `toLinearIfView` converts for the HDR path. So today the sun has the brightness of a texture: at most 1.0, the same as a white wall, which is why bloom cannot treat it as a light source. **Verify** the quad's local size (`sDrawQuad`) to find the disc's angular size; with scale 450 at distance 1000 the sun is far larger than a real sun (about half a degree), because it is a game object, not a physical one.

The sun's position comes from `WeatherManager::update`: a half circle from `mSunriseTime` to `mTimeSettings.mNightStart`, tilted by about 15 degrees (`-0.268` in y), and the model takes its height from the same vector (`Render::Atmosphere::sunElevation`). The colour tables (`TimeOfDayInterpolator::getValue`) blend sunrise, day, sunset and night colours in windows centred on `mSunriseTime` and `mSunsetTime`, so they say "sunset" at about 17:30 to 19:30 (defaults `Weather_Sunset_Time`, `Weather_Sunset_Duration`; **verify** the numbers from the user's `Morrowind.ini`) while the drawn sun reaches the horizon at `mNightStart`, 20:00 by default. Task 0034 measured the gap: the drawn sun is about 30 degrees high at 17:30.

## Design, in plain language

**The disc.** A real sun is a bright disc that is dimmer at its edge than in its middle ("limb darkening", because we see cooler, shallower layers at the rim). We compute for each pixel of the sun quad its distance `r` from the centre in units of the disc radius, and give it:

- the disc: intensity `I(r) = 1 - u (1 - sqrt(1 - r^2))` for `r < 1`, a standard one-parameter limb darkening law (u about 0.6, grey-ish; the law and the constant are from the astronomy literature, **verify** the citation at stage a), zero outside, with a soft edge of a configurable width so it does not alias or shimmer;
- the corona and glow around it: a sum of two falloffs, a tight bright one and a wide faint one, `glow(r) = a / (1 + (r / w1)^2) + b * exp(-r / w2)`, with the amount scaled by the glow setting and by the air (more glow, redder, when the sun is low or the air hazy), because the glow is light scattered by the air and particles between us and the sun. These shapes, not the texture, are the "glow curve" and "edge softness" of ENB's parameter list;
- the colour: the weather's sun colour (after task 0034's model, so physical when `atmosphere` is on), and the **intensity in HDR**: the disc is many times brighter than white (the multiplier setting; a real sun is about 100000 times a white surface, which we do not do; a value in the tens is enough for bloom to treat it as a light), the glow a small fraction of that. With `hdr` and `hdr bloom` on, bloom then spreads the sun naturally. Without bloom the disc just clips to white in the middle, with the colour showing only in the glow.

**Why not simply keep the texture and multiply it?** Because the texture's disc, glow and flare are baked into one picture at one size and one colour; the computed version can follow the air (redder and larger-looking at the horizon), the weather (hidden by cloud and fog through the existing `glareView`) and the hour, and it stays sharp at any resolution. It also removes a modded-texture dependency. The old textured sun stays as the fallback when the setting is off.

**What is kept.** The occlusion query, the flash and the glare overlay (`SunGlare`) still run: they are what makes the sun flash when it comes out from behind a tree, and they are Morrowind's behaviour. In the first build the flash texture stays as it is; the glare overlay colour stays the INI's.

**Following the colour tables' hour (decision 4).** Morrowind's colour tables go to sunset colours well before the sun touches the horizon, so with a physical model driven by the sun's real height the sunset warmth arrives late (task 0034, stage c). toddhow's answer is to follow the tables' hour. The way to do it without moving the sun: define a **table elevation**, a smooth function of the game hour that is 0 at the middle of each table window (the sunrise and sunset windows used by `TimeOfDayInterpolator`), reaches its maximum at midday, and goes negative at night, shaped like a sine so that it has the physically correct slope near the horizon. The atmosphere model, the disc colour and the disc's glow are driven by this table elevation instead of by the drawn sun's height. The drawn sun and the light direction (shadows) are not moved, so no gameplay-feel change.

The honest consequence: for the last hours of the day the disc may show sunset colours while it is still some height above the horizon, as in the vanilla game (the vanilla sun is also orange then). That is the vanilla look and is what "follow the tables" means. If toddhow would rather have the drawn sun's orbit itself touch the horizon at the tables' sunset (so the picture is consistent and shadows lengthen at the right time), that is a separate option (D4) because it changes where shadows fall at every hour.

## Maths (stage a), `components/render/sunmath.hpp`, namespace `Render::Sun`, no game types

All values are dimensionless unless stated. The twin is `proceduralsun.slang`; a unit test compares the two on a grid via the CPU functions only (no GPU in tests).

- `float limbDarkening(float r, float u)`: `1 - u * (1 - sqrt(1 - r*r))` for `0 <= r <= 1`, 0 outside, with `r` clamped and NaN giving 0. Monotone decreasing in `r`; `u = 0` is a flat disc; the value at the centre is 1.
- `float edgeCoverage(float r, float radius, float softness)`: `1 - smoothstep(radius - softness, radius + softness, r)`, 1 inside, 0 outside, so the edge has no jaggies at any size.
- `float glowFalloff(float r, float radius, GlowParams p)`: the sum of the two falloffs, continuous at the disc edge, always non-negative, integrates to a finite amount (tested numerically), zero amount when the glow scale is 0.
- `vec3 sunDiscColour(vec3 sunColour, float intensity, float limb)`: the HDR colour of a disc pixel.
- `float tableElevation(float gameHour, TimeOfDayWindows w)`: 0 at the middle of the sunrise window and of the sunset window, maximum at the midpoint of the day, a sine in between so it is smooth, and negative (a mirrored sine) at night; continuous across midnight; tested at every window edge. `TimeOfDayWindows` is built by the weather manager from `mTimeSettings` (the same numbers `TimeOfDayInterpolator` uses), so mods that change the INI keep working.
- `float glowRedness(float elevation, float clearness)`: how much more the glow is tinted red near the horizon (uses the atmosphere model's transmittance, `Render::Atmosphere::sunTransmittance`, rather than a second formula).
- `bool useProceduralSun(bool setting, bool hdr)`.

## Stages

- **a. Maths and tests.** `sunmath.hpp`, `testsun.cpp`, written from this spec first (a separate test-writing agent), then implemented. No renderer change; no frame comparison needed.
- **b. The disc.** `proceduralsun.slang` added to `files/shaders/CMakeLists.txt` and the SPIR-V declaration test; `Sun` chooses the pipeline when the setting is on; frames of the sun at noon, 17:30, 19:30 and sunrise in clear weather, looked at, with the setting off equal to the commit before by `diffmap`.
- **c. HDR intensity and bloom.** The intensity setting, a frame check with `hdr bloom` on and off, the default value chosen by eye, a check that the sun is not drawn bright through walls (depth is off in the sky bin; the existing query handles occlusion) and that the occlusion query still gives the same `visibleRatio` (the query uses the same quad).
- **d. The hour.** `tableElevation` computed in `WeatherManager::update`; the atmosphere model and the disc driven by it; frames at 17:30, 19:00 and 19:45 against the colour tables' look; the drawn sun, shadows and light direction unchanged (diff of a shadow frame).
- **e. Moons (optional).** Only if cheap and if toddhow approves the first four.
- **f. Notes.** Cost (a few fragment operations on one quad: expected under 0.05 ms), module guide entry, settings documented.

## Constraints

- Off by default; with `procedural sun = false` the sun is drawn exactly as before and the frame equals the commit before by `diffmap`. With `atmosphere` off the table elevation is not used. No new descriptor set or uniform: the disc's parameters travel in the existing material (`emissive` for colour and intensity, the spare `pbr` vec4 for limb, glow and radius) so `Pipeline::Data::Scene` (the 640 byte hazard) does not grow. **Verify** `Material`'s spare fields are free for the sun (the sun's material is its own object; `sun.slang` has a copy of the layout that must match).
- No allocation per frame; the table elevation is a handful of floats per frame.
- Determinism: a pure function of the game hour and the weather; no wall clock, no randomness.
- New shader files are Slang and are listed in `files/shaders/CMakeLists.txt`; check a frame after every shader change (`apps/openmw/mwrender/CLAUDE.md`: shader changes have blanked every frame before on this machine's driver).
- The editor keeps building and drawing; it has no sky, so it does not take this.
- The occlusion query, flash and glare keep working and keep their order of bins.
