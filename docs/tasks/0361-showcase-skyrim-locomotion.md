## What the evidence shows

Showcase harness `tools/showcase_check` (group `anim`, new), 1,600 x 900, fixed frame time, `[Video] framerate limit = 20` (so that 0.1 simulation seconds is 0.3 real seconds and the screenshot queue, at most 8 copies, keeps up: at 60 it skipped about 100 of 210 frames and `rename.py` then misnamed the rest), a side camera (`tp = { 0, 50, 70, 260 }`, the new fourth number is the sideways offset), 10 pictures a second. New harness aids: `turn`, `stopAt` (shots), `tp` side offset.

1. **(Corrected in the follow-up below: the body is built in the first frame after the new game's 25 to 32 s cell load, not 33 s later; the audit's pictures were wrong for another reason or were taken from behind.) The audit's walk and run frames were taken before the player's Skyrim body existed, and from behind.** The pack builds the player body only after its actor records are read on a worker (`SkyrimWorldView::playerBody` answers revision 0 until then): `PLAYERBODY built` is logged 33 s after the start in the run measured (23:07:24 for a start at 23:06:51). The harness shot at 8 s real. Until then the player is drawn as the stub with Morrowind's parts and Morrowind's animation system (the `missing: runstart, walkstop` line of task 0108 belongs to that body), which is what "after the first step the pose hardly changes" saw. A camera straight behind a walking person cannot show a leg cycle either. With the body built and a side view the run is a readable cycle (frames `keep/fid_on` p_run, p_runstop, p_runturn).
2. **The gait phase advances and the clips play** (`OPENEDEN_NEUTRAL_LOCO_TRACE`, which now names the actor): for the player, 26 to 30 lines per shot, `phase` advancing by `cps` a second, one gait at a time. What differs is the SPEED: the player's ground speed is Morrowind's. Walk: mean 149 units/s (sd 20, range 95 to 182) against the clip's 82.45, so the walk cycle plays at 1.77 times its natural rate; run: 260 to 272 against 350.87, rate 0.75. The speed also jitters 130 to 180 from sample to sample at 20 frames a second (position over frame time; the 0.12 s smoothing absorbs most of it).
3. **The walk gave way to the run only at 216 units/s (the half way speed)**, so a walk at 200 played at 2.4 times its rate; the geometric mean (170) plays walk and run at the same ratio.
4. **Skyrim has no start or stop clips.** Survey of `Skyrim - Animations.bsa`, `actors/character/animations/<male|female>/`: `mt_idle`, `mt_walk*`, `mt_run*` (forward, backward, left, right and four diagonals), `mt_sprintforward`, `npc_turnleft90`, `npc_turnleft180`, `npc_turnright90`, `npc_turnright180`, `npc_turntowalkleft90/180`, `npc_turntowalkright90/180`, and the idle variants `mt_idle_a_*`. No `walkstart`, `walkstop`, `runstart` or `runstop`: the game's graph blends idle and cycle by speed, which is what `Gait::weights` does (the idle fades out between 5 and 30 units/s, 0.12 s smoothing); the stop frames (`p_runstop`) show the figure back at the idle in about 0.4 s. The turn clips exist but need a turn-in-place state; not built here.
5. **Guards were never in the same idle phase**: the idle's start is `SceneryKeyframes::phase(hash(ref id))` (task 0145). What makes a row of guards "indistinguishable" is the clip: `mt_idle` is a very slow sway (over 20 s of pictures the standing player does not visibly change), so a different phase of the same sway is no difference.

## What is built

- **Idle fidgets** (`[Game] neutral actor idle fidgets`, default true, Skyrim people only; `components/havokpack/idlefidget.*`): a standing person plays one of nine one-shot variants of the game (`mt_idle_a_sway_fast`, `_leg_shift`, `_shoulders`, `_soft_right`, `_left_long`, `_sigh_var1`, `_sigh_var2`, `_shakeout`, `mt_idlelookingb`) over its idle with a 0.35 s fade in and out, the first 2 to 10 s in and then one every 12 to 24 s; variant and schedule come from the reference alone (splitmix64, no clock, no random numbers), so a row of guards does not move as one and a visit repeats. Not while walking (the variant takes the idle's share of the blend only).
- **Walk to run at 170** (same setting): `Gait::Params::mSwitchSpeed` (0 = the half way speed, so every older caller and test is unchanged), `Gait::balancedSwitchSpeed` = sqrt(walk speed x run speed). Creatures keep their own rule.
- Trace: `NLOCO <actor>` and `NFIDGET <actor>` lines (`OPENEDEN_NEUTRAL_LOCO_TRACE`).

Constraints: pure functions, nothing allocates per frame (the variant's clip is decoded once on the idle's workers and cached per skeleton, sex and clip name), deterministic.

## Follow-up of 2026-11-20: toddhow's two answers

1. **"Read the records sooner so the player's Skyrim body appears at once."** The measurement says the body was never late: in the run, `Starting a new game` 18:14:41.46, first Skyrim actor built 18:15:04.2, `PLAYERBODY built` 18:15:06.88, first shot 18:15:10: the 25 s are the new game's cell load (it blocks the main thread; a loading screen covers it), and the body is built in the first frame after it. The earlier "33 s" of this doc was wall time since the process start. What WAS slow is that the actor records were read on the main thread inside that load: the combat worker that reads them (task 0171's) started only when the body first polled (`playerBody()`, once frames run), so the first NPC's `ensureActorIndex` read every plugin again on the main thread (the gap in the log before the first actor: 18:15:02.578 to 18:15:04.217, 1.64 s). Now, with the player body on, `SkyrimWorldView::loadCell` (main thread) starts that worker at the first cell build, and `ensureActorIndex` adopts its result when it has been started (`combatReady()` adopts on the main thread) instead of reading; a notice `actor records read on the main thread in N ms` names the old path. After: `combat records read on a worker in 3,334 ms (the main thread did not wait)`, no main-thread read line, gap before the first actor 0.16 s. Total load time: 25.4 s before, 32.2 s after in the single runs, a spread I cannot attribute (the machine was shared; the two runs differ by more than the 1.5 s saved), so I claim only the main-thread stall. Without the player body nothing is read ahead (a test pins both). Tests: `SkyrimSeEarlyActorIndex` (apps/components_tests/skyrimse/testskyrimseactorindexearly.cpp): both red against the old code, both green.
2. **"Use Skyrim's walk and run speeds for the player in Skyrim sessions."** `[Game] neutral player speeds` (default true, in the test profile and README; needs `[Game] neutral world`): the player's walk is 82.45 and his run 350.87 units a second (`MWPhysics::NeutralPath::kWalkSpeed`/`kRunSpeed`, the mover's and the gait's numbers). `MWWorld::neutralPlayerSpeed` (apps/openmw/mwworld/neutralplayerspeed.hpp, pure) decides; `MWClass::Npc::getWalkSpeed` and `getRunSpeed` apply it for the player only when the session is neutral (so the inertial locomotion's run-entry test, which compares the speed with `getWalkSpeed`, Lua's `walkSpeed` and the AI see the same numbers: **a first version in `getMaxSpeed` alone made the run unreachable with inertial locomotion on, the run share stayed 0**); sneaking keeps its multiplier on the walk; swimming and levitating keep the Morrowind numbers through their own branches (swimming uses the new walk and run numbers as its base); encumbrance 0 speed stays 0. Measured on the player trace (`NLOCO player`, `anim` group, 20 fps limit, teleport frames and the first two samples left out): walk mean 78 (28 to 95), cycle rate 0.84 of natural (was 1.77 times); run mean 310 including the ramp, steady up to 354, rate about 1.0 (was 0.75). Frames `keep/sheets/sheetC_p_walk.png`, `_p_run`, `_p_runstop`, `_p_runturn` (30 pictures at 0.1 s): the run is a clear stride cycle. Morrowind: the `getMaxSpeed`/walk/run code is unchanged for every session without `neutral world` (the setting is read first).

## Decisions for toddhow

1. The load of a Skyrim new game takes 25 to 32 s on this machine, all on the main thread; that is what looked like "the body appears after 30 s". Making the load shorter (the cell build, the model reads) is a streaming task (0357 and its remaining steps), not this one.
2. The player's Skyrim walk is 82.45, slower than Morrowind's 149 and than Skyrim's own jog: with "always run" off the player walks at the clip's speed. Set `[Game] neutral player speeds = false` to go back.
3. The idle variants and the 170 switch are behind `[Game] neutral actor idle fidgets` (default true, Skyrim people only); false gives the build before.
