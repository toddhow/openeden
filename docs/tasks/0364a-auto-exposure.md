## Design

- **Measure (`post/autoexposure.slang`, `Render::AutoExposurePass`):** three dispatches after the scene and the TAA overlay, before bloom and the tonemap. `clearMain` zeroes a 66-word device-local buffer; `histMain` takes one sample every `stride` pixels (at least 4; the stride grows so that no more than 2^19 samples are taken: 129,600 at 1080p), each a 2 by 2 bilinear average of the HDR colour, and counts its Rec. 709 luminance into a 64-bin log2 histogram (bin 0: too dark to count, 63 bins over -8 .. +8 stops) in group-shared memory first, then once per group into the global one; it also sums the luminance, clamped at 16, in 1/256 fixed point (the arithmetic mean, for the grade pivot); `resolveMain` (one thread) computes the trimmed mean between the low and high percentile and writes it, the arithmetic mean, the weight and the frame's serial into one of 8 slots of a host-visible ring.
- **Read (no waiting):** the CPU reads the ring each frame and takes the newest slot at least 4 frames old (`pickSlot`; the frame fence ring is three long, so such a frame has finished). A slot torn by a frame in flight is impossible for the slot chosen, and a torn float only mixes two neighbouring frames.
- **Adapt (`Render::AutoExposure::State`):** target stops = `log2(key) - meanLog2` clamped to `[min, max]`; the current value moves by `1 - exp(-rate * dt)` of the way (rate `speed up` when the exposure rises, i.e. the scene went dark, `speed down` when it falls), dt clamped to 0.5 s. The first measurement of a session is taken as it is; after that the exposure follows at the set speeds, also across a door, a teleport or a loading screen (a long frame counts as at most 0.5 s), so stepping from a cellar into the sun dazzles for a moment and settles, as the eye does.
- **Apply:** the exposure change is added to the tonemap's `exposureStops` exactly as the weather's image-space brightness is (a delta against what was applied), so the manual `hdr exposure` and the Skyrim image-space and night exposure lift stay as compensation on top of it. A post-processing technique that sets `hdr` (tonemaps for itself) switches it off.
- **Pivot:** with a colour grade whose contrast is not 1 (the weather image space of a Skyrim session), `gradeApply` used 36 texture reads per pixel to find the scene's mean brightness (task 0362a). With auto exposure on, the pass's arithmetic mean luminance goes through the same exposure, curve, clip and encode on the CPU (`pivotFromMeanLuma`) and is passed in the `w` of the existing `gradeTint` push constant (0 = not measured: the shader keeps its 36 taps). Same push constant size, same descriptor sets; the tonemap's interface records did not change.
- **What was not built (and why):** *Pre-exposure in the sense of writing the scene in exposed units.* The scene target is RGBA16F (range 65,504) and every shader writes the unexposed linear value; scaling it at the lit shaders would touch every family (and TAA history, bloom and the G-buffer's ambient output) and needs the physical sky (stage D) to have any use. The value the sky will need is available (`RenderManager`'s tonemap exposure) and the pass measures before the tonemap, so the later change is a scale factor in the scene block (which has no free slot today: it cannot grow past 640 bytes). Not done in A1.
