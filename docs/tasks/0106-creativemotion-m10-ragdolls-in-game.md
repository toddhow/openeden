Design doc section: CreativeMotion `CLAUDE.md`, "Path to OpenMW", M10; `docs/tasks/0035-creativemotion-m10-physics-adapter.md` (the adapter, the layer, `RagdollHost`, the scale fix: all built); `docs/tasks/0065-creativemotion-push-sandbox.md` (the editor's use of the same adapter); `docs/decisions/0001-creativemotion-compiled-into-the-engine.md`, `docs/decisions/0003-plugins-do-not-change-physics-or-lua.md`, `docs/decisions/0005` (no save data for extensions; CreativeMotion is core).
Status: written 2026-11-10 (toddhow said "Yes" to starting M10 on 2026-11-10). Full loop (anything under `apps/openmw/mwphysics/` and the CreativeMotion adapters). Commit at the time of writing: `846dbb8849`.
Goal: with `[Physics] creativemotion ragdolls = true`, a non-player humanoid NPC that dies becomes a CreativeMotion ragdoll in the game's single Jolt world, and the ragdoll's pose drives the NPC's skeleton each frame, so the corpse falls and settles on the world instead of playing the canned death clip's final pose.

## Design
- **One Jolt world, one adapter.** Actor ragdolls live in the same `RagdollHost` / `HostedJoltWorld` as the debug ragdoll. At most `cMaxActorRagdolls` (8) at once; a ninth death keeps its death animation (logged once at Verbose). Handles are `{index, generation}`; a stale handle fails every call.
- **Trigger.** In `CharacterController::postAnimation`, main thread: the actor is dead (`isDead()`), is an NPC (`mNpc`), is not the player (the player's death reloads the game), the controller saw it alive earlier in its life (a corpse loaded from a save is not ragdolled: it lies in the death clip's last pose, which is not a standing pose), a `PhysicsSystem` exists, the pelvis bone exists in the actor and at least ten of the 17 skeleton-profile bones can be driven (a rig bone with no body, like the neck in the embedded humanoid rig, or an engine bone the actor lacks, is skipped and keeps the animation's local rotation under its driven parent) (`components/creativemotion/data/profiles/humanoid-to-eden.skeletonprofile.json`; the table is repeated in the driver), and the setting is on.
- **Build.** The embedded humanoid rig at the actor's feet (`Ptr` position, which is at the feet) and yaw, limp (`makeLimp`), every body given the actor's inertial velocity (`Actor::getInertialForce`, units per second, converted at the host's named boundary). The rig is in its standing rest pose; the actor's bones are NOT posed into the ragdoll (see "Limits"). Layers: `RAGDOLL` meets `WORLD`, `HEIGHTMAP`, `DOOR`, `RAGDOLL` only, so a ragdoll never meets any actor capsule, projectile or sensor, and existing queries never see it.
- **Collision of the corpse's own character controller.** Unchanged: the dead actor's capsule is on `ACTOR` and, after the death clip, `DEBRIS` (`Actors::update` -> `World::enableActorCollision(false)` -> `Actor::enableCollisionBody`). Neither layer meets `RAGDOLL`, so nothing needs to be removed; the capsule keeps resting where the actor died and is still what picking, activation and looting use.
- **Driving the skeleton.** Each frame, after the animation, the manual bone rotations and the IK controller (all in `postAnimation`, main thread), the driver reads every ragdoll body (`readActorRagdoll`, no allocation) and writes the mapped bones that have both a body and an engine bone (16 with the embedded rig: it has no neck body) parent first, through `BonePose` only. For bone `i`: `world rotation = ragdollBoneRotation(t) * delta_i`, where `delta_i = inverse(ragdollBoneRotation(0)) * actorBoneWorldRotation(0)` was captured on the spawn frame (so the first frame draws exactly the pose the animation had: no pop), and the local rotation is `inverse(parentWorldRotation) * world rotation`. The pelvis bone's origin follows the ragdoll pelvis's displacement since the spawn (world, in units, written as a local translation under its parent). Unmapped bones (clavicles, fingers, toes, hair, weapons, `Bip01` itself) keep playing the death clip. The animation is not stopped, so its text keys, sounds and end-of-death handling are as before.
- **Units.** Metres only inside the adapter and the rig. `RagdollHost` converts at its two named functions (`toMetres` for the spawn pose and velocity, `fromMetres` for the bone readback); the driver only sees game units.
- **Threads.** `startActorRagdoll`, `readActorRagdoll`, `stopActorRagdoll`, `RagdollHost::beginStep/endStep` and the driver all run on the main thread (`World::update`, `CharacterController::postAnimation`). Spawn and removal take `PhysicsTaskScheduler::getSimulationMutex()` like `spawnDebugRagdoll`, because the actor job may be querying. The adapter registers no Jolt callback; nothing runs on a Jolt worker. Ragdoll bodies are outside every mask the actor job uses.
- **Allocation.** `beginStep`, `endStep`, `readActorRagdoll` and the driver's per-frame `apply` allocate nothing (preallocated state vectors; bone handles found once). Spawning allocates (once per death).
- **Determinism.** No clock, no randomness; the same call sequence on the same build gives the same bodies (adapter test, task 0035). Which frame the ragdoll starts on depends on the game, as the death does.
- **Lifetimes.** The ragdoll is removed when: the actor is removed from the physics system (`PhysicsSystem::remove`, disable, delete, cell unload), its controller rebuilds its bone controllers in a live game (`createPoseControllers`: new bones, so the driver ends with the ragdoll), it falls `cRagdollKillDepthMetres` below its spawn, or the physics system is destroyed. The controller's destructor and `detachAnimation` only drop the driver and never call into the physics system, because the world can already be gone then (a quickload clears the world first); the ragdoll leaves with its actor through `PhysicsSystem::remove`, which `Scene::removeObjectFromScene` and the cell unload always call, or with the host at shutdown. A driver whose handle is stale stops writing bones and is dropped; the bones then keep the last pose they were given until the animation writes them again.
- **Setting.** `[Physics] creativemotion ragdolls` (existing, false). With it false: no `RagdollHost` is created for actors, no Jolt body of ours exists, the death path executes one bool test per frame of a dead NPC, and frames and physics are identical to the parent executable (checked, below).
- **Saves.** Not saved. A ragdolled corpse's pose is not in the save (decision 0005). On load the actor is a corpse in the death clip's last pose (the clip's final frame, as without ragdolls). A save made while a ragdoll is still settling therefore shows the corpse in its death-clip pose after the load. The actor's `Ptr` position never changes (the capsule does not follow the ragdoll), so the saved position is where the actor died.

## Limits of this slice (stated, not hidden)
1. The ragdoll starts in the rig's standing rest pose, not in the actor's pose at the moment of death. The rotation binding hides the pop, but a limb that the animation had raised starts from "rest rotation plus the physics' change from rest". Posing the rig to the actor's pose (a swing per bone, joint limits re-centred) is the next slice.
2. Only the pelvis translates; limb lengths are the skeleton's, not the rig's. The rig's body sizes are the CreativeMotion humanoid's, not the actor's race (beast races and tall or short NPCs use the same rig).
3. The corpse's capsule, activation box and hit boxes stay where the actor died; a ragdoll that slides far (a slope) draws away from its loot hitbox. A ragdoll that leaves the visible bounds may be culled with the actor's node.
4. Creatures, the player, summoned creatures and actors with a missing bone get no ragdoll.
5. No settling hand-off: when a ragdoll sleeps the driver keeps writing its (constant) pose each frame; it is removed only by the lifetime events above.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] (a) `RagdollHost` hosts up to 8 actor ragdolls beside the debug ragdoll in one adapter; spawn / read / remove are allocation-free in read and step, stale handles fail, the ninth is refused with a message, removal leaves the Jolt body count as found (unit tests, `openmw-tests`).
- [ ] (a) With the setting off no `RagdollHost` exists for actors and `stepSimulation` runs the same two null tests (unit test of the null path through `PhysicsSystem` helpers or the existing hosting tests).
- [ ] (b) The rotation maths: at the spawn frame the driven bone rotation equals the actor's; a ragdoll rotation `Q` applied to the body gives `Q * actor rotation` in world space; local = inverse(parent) * world round-trips (`ragdollbonemath.hpp`, unit tests incl. random rotations).
- [ ] (b) `ActorRagdollDriver::apply` writes the mapped bones through `BonePose` from a fake ragdoll source: at spawn the bone world rotations are unchanged, a body turned by 90 degrees turns its bone by 90 degrees about the same world axis, unmapped bones are untouched, a stale source ends the driver, no allocation per frame.
- [ ] (b) A dead NPC with the setting on gets exactly one ragdoll; a corpse created dead gets none; the player and creatures get none; removal on `PhysicsSystem::remove` (tests on the pure decision helper and the PhysicsSystem-side map).
- [ ] (c) In a quiet-window game, an NPC killed by script falls and settles: frames over 3 s viewed (no explosion, no sinking), the lowest ragdoll body stays above the floor spawn height minus a tolerance, settle time and top speed logged; two runs with `OPENEDEN_FIXED_FRAME_TIME` log identical final poses where the death is repeatable.
- [ ] With the setting off, the same scene's frames are byte-identical to the parent executable's and the Jolt body count is equal.
Out of scope: posing the ragdoll to the actor, creatures, the player, balance and get-up (M11), partial ragdolls (hit reactions), saves of the pose, cell-unload hand-off to a frozen pose, activation hitboxes following the ragdoll, Lua access, extension access (decision 0003), Eden Studio.

## Constraints
Full loop. One Jolt world (the adapter takes `JPH::PhysicsSystem&`). No heap allocation in per-step and per-frame code. Layer rules stay at 104 exceptions or lower. `components/creativemotion/` is not edited. clang-format 14 and `-Werror` clean. Settings: no new setting; the existing one is already in `tools/testprofile/` (checked in the stage log).

## Stage log

### Stages a, b and c (2026-11-10; same hand wrote tests and code, no fresh reviewer; Windows only; local commits `d64e2a43a7`, `c679d5a81f` and the commit of this log, not pushed)
Plain words: with `[Physics] creativemotion ragdolls = true`, a non-player NPC that dies in the game falls as a CreativeMotion ragdoll in the game's one Jolt world and its skeleton follows the ragdoll, instead of ending in the death clip's last pose. Nothing else changes; with the setting off no ragdoll body exists.

**Built.** (a) `RagdollHost` (task 0035's) hosts up to 8 actor ragdolls beside the debug one: `spawnActor`, `readActor`, `removeActor`, `hasActor`, `actorStatus`, generation-checked handles (`ragdolltypes.hpp`), a kill depth for them (checked every 30 steps), the rig and profile parsed once; capacity 64 to 192 bodies and joints; `metres()` and `fromMetres()` are its only unit conversions. `PhysicsSystem::startActorRagdoll`, `readActorRagdoll`, `stopActorRagdoll`, `hasActorRagdoll`, `actorRagdollBones`, `actorRagdollCount`, a map keyed by the actor's `mRef`, and `PhysicsSystem::remove` ends the actor's ragdoll (one lookup when the map is empty). (b) `MWRender::ActorRagdollDriver` (+ `ragdollbonemath.hpp`), `MWMechanics::shouldStartActorRagdoll` (`ragdolltrigger.hpp`) and `CharacterController::updateRagdoll`, called last in `postAnimation`. (c) `tools/ragdoll_check/` (a Lua scene that kills the nearest NPC and takes nine frames), the developer trace `OPENEDEN_RAGDOLL_TRACE` (every 2 s each actor ragdoll's lowest and highest body, top speed and sleep state, and the step at which every body first sleeps), the test-profile lines (the setting was already in `profile.cfg` from task 0035; its comment and the README line now mention the death ragdolls).

**Findings while building.** (1) The embedded humanoid rig has 16 bodies and **none for the neck**: a driver that required all 17 mapped bones would have been refused on every real death (it passed every unit test with a fake that had 17). The driver now skips a mapped bone with no body or no engine bone (the neck keeps the animation's local rotation under its driven chest; the pelvis is required, at least ten bones must be driven), and one test builds the driver from a real `RagdollHost` over the fake Morrowind skeleton. (2) **`vsg::dquat`'s `*` is OSG's order: `a * b` applies `a` FIRST.** The first draft of the maths used the textbook order and four driver tests failed; the pure functions now go through one `compose(outer, inner)` and say so (`ragdollbonemath.hpp`). (3) GCC `-Werror` found one `-Wrange-loop-construct` in two test files (the pitfall in the root guide); fixed. (4) The actor's own `Ptr` position never changes, so picking, activation and looting use the capsule where the actor died (limit 3).

**Tests.** 46 new tests in `openmw-tests`: `ActorRagdolls` (host: handles, the ninth refused, removal, stale handles, yaw and pelvis placement, velocity, settles on a floor, two runs bit-identical, kill depth, allocation of read and step: 0), `ActorRagdollDriver` (the real rig, the spawn frame draws the animation's pose, a quarter turn turns every mapped bone the same way, a bone with no body keeps its local rotation, unmapped bones untouched, the pelvis follows the displacement, a gone ragdoll ends the driver, `apply` allocates 0), `RagdollBoneMath` (random rotations), `RagdollTrigger` (all 256 flag combinations: exactly one accepts). **They were written AFTER the code, so none was run red against a stub** (a deviation from the loop; a stub run was not done). Instead, six single-line mutations were applied to the built code, each rebuilt and run, and each was caught by named tests: readActor without the body-in-bone offset (3 tests), the pelvis translation off (2), the kill depth never firing (1), the compose order wrong (6), the generation not bumped (1), the trigger ignoring "loaded dead" (2). `openmw-tests`: 1,777 of 1,777 passed at `c679d5a81f`.

**In game** (`tools/ragdoll_check/check.sh`, Seyda Neen with the user's Morrowind data from a copy of a config, quiet window, `OPENEDEN_FIXED_FRAME_TIME=0.0166667`, `--random-seed 1`, the nearest NPC (an Imperial Guard on a boardwalk) killed by a Lua local script 1.5 s after the player is placed 450 units south of it): exit 0, no crash dump, no error line, nine frames, one ragdoll started. Frames viewed (`I:\Projects\OpenEden-work\m10\runs\new_on1\frames`, and an earlier real-time run at 250 units): the guard crumples in the first 0.5 s, falls forward and lies on the boardwalk end by 2 s; no explosion, no stretching, no sinking through the planks. Numbers (trace): the ragdoll bodies' lowest and highest centre 237.3 and 258.5 units after settling (spawn height 241.2), top speed 0.22 m/s at 2 s, 0.29 at 4 s, 0.10 at 6 s, 0.23 at 8 s, 0.11 at 10 s, **every body asleep after 676 steps (11.27 s)** and 0 m/s after; the unit test on a flat floor sleeps within 8 s, so the slope and the planks lengthen it. **Two runs give the identical trace (every logged number equal)**; their frames are not byte-identical (next point).

**Off means unchanged (as far as measured).** With the setting off the same scene runs and the NPC dies as before (`ragdolls started 0`, no error); a death costs one bool test per frame until the first frame that sees it, then none. **Frames are not byte-identical in any pair, the parent executable included:** parent against parent differs by 6,186 to 15,507 pixels of 480,000 (channel difference over 4), the new executable with the setting off against the parent by 374 to 10,177 and against itself by 639 to 19,136; the differences seen were a tree trunk at the left edge and walking NPCs (streaming and animation timing, cause not investigated), not the corpse. So "off is identical" is claimed for the code path (nothing runs; no body is created), not for frames. The Jolt body count with the setting off was not compared in game. The parent executable was built from `846dbb8849` in the same build folder (`openmw-parent.exe`).

**Threads (each callback).** `CharacterController::updateRagdoll` and `ActorRagdollDriver::apply`: main thread, in `postAnimation`, after the animation threads have joined. `PhysicsSystem::startActorRagdoll` and `stopActorRagdoll`: main thread, taking `PhysicsTaskScheduler::getSimulationMutex()` (the actor job may be querying; bodies are added and removed under it). `PhysicsSystem::remove`: main thread (`Scene`), calls `stopActorRagdoll`. `RagdollHost::readActor` and `actorStatus`: main thread, through the adapter's locking `BodyInterface` (thread safe). `RagdollHost::beginStep` and `endStep`: main thread, around `PhysicsSystem::Update` in `stepSimulation`. The adapter registers no Jolt callback; nothing runs on a Jolt worker.

**Limits found beyond the spec's list.** A death against a wall spawns the standing bodies inside static geometry (the ragdoll is built in the rig's standing pose at the feet; Jolt separates them). A ragdoll started while the NPC is knocked down or swimming is still the standing rig. The kill depth is 20 m below the spawn. A rebuild of the actor's bones (werewolf, camera reattachment) ends the ragdoll and the corpse snaps back to the death clip.

**Not run / not verified.** The Linux gate beyond GCC 13 `-Werror -fsyntax-only` of the changed `.cpp` files and the new test files (clean); clang-tidy; sanitizers; the 32 Lua integration tests; `components-tests`; a fresh reviewing agent; the editor (`eden-studio` not rebuilt); a creature, a summoned creature, an NPC in an interior, a corpse looted while the ragdoll is awake, a quickload with a live ragdoll, a cell unload with one, resurrection (the `resetCurrentDeathState` hook) in game; eight ragdolls at once (host unit-tested only); the frame cost of a ragdoll (not measured); the activation box staying behind. ### Second slice (2026-11-10; same hand wrote tests and code, no fresh reviewer; Windows only; local commits on branch `m10b`, not pushed)
Plain words: the four limits of the first slice that could be fixed are fixed, behind the same off-by-default `[Physics] creativemotion ragdolls`. (1) The ragdoll starts in the actor's pose at death (and moving as the actor moved). (2) A ragdoll built in a wall or floor is moved out first. (3) Once the ragdoll sleeps the corpse (capsule, saved position, culling bounds) is moved to where it lies. (4) Creatures: left out.

**(1) Pose at death.** `ActorRagdollRest` (the 17 mapped bones' model-space rotations, read in `createPoseControllers`, setting on and NPC only), `makeActorStartPose`, `RagdollBones::restRelative`, `ActorStartPose`, `RagdollHost::spawnActor(..., pose)`. The rig's bones are turned to the actor's pose relative to rest (a bone nobody names turns with its parent) and its root is placed so the rig pelvis is on the actor's pelvis; the driver is unchanged (its captured offset is now exactly the rest rotation). Joint limits are the rig's, measured from the start pose, NOT re-centred on anatomy (a bent elbow can bend the rig's range further either way); the rig's limb lengths are not the skeleton's. Assumption stated: the rig's rest pose and the Morrowind skeleton's rest pose are taken as the same posture (frame offsets come from the rest snapshot); a residual posture difference shows as the rig's own. Velocity: `ActorVelocityTracker` (position per frame while alive, smoothed 0.5, teleports clear it, cut to 12 m/s) replaces the physics inertia, which is zero for an actor walking on the ground. Tests first: 12 of 18 host/driver pose tests red against stubs (two of them were also red from a dangling reference in the test itself, fixed), 4 of 7 velocity tests red; mutations (local-rotation formula, restRelative order, shift direction, corpse min-shift `&&`) each caught by named tests.
**(2) Wall/floor.** `RagdollHost::findStartShift`: the posed bodies are collided (CollideShape, back faces ignored) against WORLD, HEIGHTMAP, DOOR; the whole ragdoll moves out along the deepest contact, at most 8 passes and 2 m in all, the least bad place seen is kept (a block with no way out gives shift zero), one Info line when it moved. 2 of 6 placement tests red first (wall, floor); a solid-block test pins that the call returns and the shift is within 2 m. **In game the wall scenario did NOT produce a contact** (the wall the NPC's rays found is at chest height only; no "start moved" line in three runs), so the wall case is proven by unit tests only.
**(3) Corpse.** `MWMechanics::decideCorpseRest` (pure: settled, once, min 0.35 m, max 40 m, ground found) and `CharacterController::placeCorpseAtRagdollRest` (every 10 frames while the ragdoll lives; ray down from the pelvis for the ground; `World::moveObject` to pelvis x,y and ground z; skipped across an exterior cell border, which would end the ragdoll). 7 of 8 pure tests red first. In game, walk scenario (guard killed at 198 u/s on the boardwalk, `tools/ragdoll_check/check.sh <build> <config> <work> on walk`, `DISTANCE=230`): the ragdoll carries on 35 units, settles after 10.98 s, "Corpse ... moved to where its ragdoll lies", the guard's `position` changed by 35.1 units, the container window opens on activation (UI mode Container, frame viewed) and a rendering ray at the corpse hits the guard. The rendering-ray pick already hit the body before (picking uses the skinned mesh); what the move changes is the physics capsule, the saved position and the culling bounds (not separately measured).
**(4) Creatures.** The only rig is CreativeMotion's humanoid (`components/creativemotion/data` holds no creature rig; `ragdollrigdata.cpp` embeds only it), so creatures keep their death clip. `shouldStartActorRagdoll` already refuses them.
**Game checks** (quiet window, fixed frame time, seed 1, Seyda Neen guard; exit 0, no dump, no error line each): still scenario settles in 5.03 s (11.27 s in slice one: the pose start is calmer); frames viewed for still, walk (old against new, `runs/cmp_walk_close.png`: both walk, crumple and lie; the new one slides on) and the loot window.
