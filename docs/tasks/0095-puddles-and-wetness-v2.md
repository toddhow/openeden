## Design, in plain language

Today a puddle is "flat enough here and a noise value is high here" (research 0049 section 3). The new idea: bake, once, for every exterior cell, a map saying how deep a puddle could be at each point if water collected there (the **capacity**), which pit it belongs to, and how much ground drains into it. At run time one number, the water supplied by the rain minus what the ground absorbed, becomes a water level inside each pit. The deepest point wets first, the level rises, the edge creeps outward along the ground's contours, and when the rain stops the level falls and the edge retreats the same way. Material decides how much the ground soaks up before any water stands. Where no map exists, the noise puddles stay as they are.

| # | Decision | What it means in the code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Capacity is measured from the pit's floor | The baker fills with no cap and calls `capacityFromFloor` (`components/puddle/puddlebake.cpp`); the per-texel cap of `fillDepressions` is not used. |
| 2 | A very deep pit holds a puddle only at its floor, up to a maximum puddle depth | The maximum is the baker's `puddle bake capacity cap`, default 12 units (about 17 cm, the spec's cap), stored in 1/16 units, at most 15.9375 (the 8 bit capacity channel). The cap is the same number as decision 1's: a pit deeper than it keeps its lowest `cap` units. |
| 3 | Minimum basin area about 16 texels | `puddle bake min area`, default 16. The area counted is the pit's POND (its texels with a stored capacity above zero after decisions 1 and 2), not the whole pit; smaller ponds are dropped before the catchment is computed. At 64 units a texel that is 16 texels = 4 x 4 = 256 x 256 units (3.6 m square). |
| 4 | A per-cell height base | The `top` channel is 16 bits at 1/8 unit above the cell's own base = the lowest texel height of the cell rounded down to a whole unit (float in the header). A cell whose own span is over 8,191.875 units clamps and is counted (`BakeStats::mHeightsClamped`). Morrowind's whole range (-2,152 to 18,952) is then covered cell by cell. |
| 5 | The catchment counts only ground within a bounded distance of the pond | `Render::Puddle::catchmentRatioBounded`: only a texel outside every pit whose water path to the pit is at most `puddle bake catchment radius` long counts as inflow. The distance is measured ALONG the flood tree (the water's path), in tenths of a texel with 10 per step along an axis and 14 per diagonal step (integers, so every compiler agrees); a path is never shorter than the straight line, so the bound is at most the radius as the crow flies. Water counts in the FIRST pit it reaches; an upstream pit's overflow is not added to the pit below (the W-a `catchmentRatio` did add it). The default radius is chosen from the measurements in the stage log. |

## Stages

Each stage is independently shippable, off by default, tests first, and ends in a commit that builds and passes the gates. **Size** is a rough effort (S under a day, M a few days, L about a week or more, in agent sessions) and **risk** is for the unknowns, both guesses.

### W-a. Pure maths (size M, risk low)
Header-only, no engine types, C++ unit tests written first and run red against stubs.
- `Render::Puddle::fillDepressions(heights, sinks, n, m)` returning filled height and capacity (priority-flood, fixed tie-break by texel index), `labelBasins` (4-connected components of capacity above 0, with area, max capacity, outlet), `accumulateFlow` (D8 on the epsilon-flooded surface) and `catchmentRatio`.
- `Render::Puddle::levelOf(freeWater, kappa, basinMax)`, `depthAt(level, basinMax, capacity)`, `coverage(depth, edge)`, `damp(level, basinMax, capacity, range)`, quantisation helpers (capacity, `log2 kappa`, height) with round-trip bounds.
- Tests on synthetic height grids with known answers:
  - a **bowl** (paraboloid, rim above its centre) fills from the bottom: depth at the centre equals the level, the zero contour is a circle of the analytic radius within one texel, the contour grows monotonically with water;
  - a **slope** (plane) has capacity 0 everywhere and drains to the sink; a tilted plane with one hollow has exactly one basin;
  - a **flat plate with a raised rim** fills evenly (every texel equal depth); a plate with no rim has capacity 0;
  - **two bowls joined at a saddle**: the lower rim spills first (the basin component and its `Cmax` are as hand-calculated);
  - a **nested pit** is merged (pins the v1 behaviour);
  - sea/sink texels get capacity 0 and drain neighbours;
  - **determinism:** the same grid gives identical bytes in repeated runs, in a transposed/rotated copy (rotated outputs equal rotated inputs' outputs), and with the order of equal-height texels permuted (index tie-break makes it independent);
  - **region independence:** splitting a large grid into tiles with a halo of one cell gives the same capacity as the whole grid for basins that lie inside a tile;
  - **properties (random grids, fixed seeds):** capacity is never negative or above the cap; `depth <= capacity`; depth is monotone in water supplied; the contour at zero water is empty; a texel with a non-ascending path to the sink has capacity 0; NaN/inf heights are refused with a located error.
- **Off means unchanged:** nothing is wired; no shader or setting exists.

## Constraints

- Everything is off by default and "off means unchanged" is compared by frames (`diffmap` at a fixed time and weather, task 0034's notes), not assumed. Default behaviour is Morrowind's.
- Derived data is baked on the user's machine from their own game data, never committed, never shipped, tests use generated land and generated scenes. A mod's own cells are baked the same way.
- Determinism: no wall clock; fixed-order priority queue with an index tie-break; no iteration over unordered containers in the baker; quantised outputs; two bakes byte-identical, also across Windows and Linux (digest excludes values that differ in the last float bit across compilers, the lesson of the 2026-11-04 `averageNormal` fix).
- No heap allocation per frame in the sampling, upload and integrator paths; the baker may allocate.
- Threading: the baker runs on the main thread in slices (as the sky-visibility baker) until task 0061; the pack loader runs on a worker and publishes to the render thread with the existing deferred-delete patterns; state which thread each new callback runs on in the pull request.
- Untrusted input: the pack is derived data that may be corrupt or from another version; the reader is strict and bounded (see W-b tests).
- No game assets, no proprietary code; the classification file contains words, not game data.
- The editor's `SceneHost` keeps building and drawing; it does not take puddles.
- Shader changes: GLSL and Slang twins change together; `SlangPermutations`, interface records and the sets lists are updated in the same change; new texture/buffer bindings are bound to dummies in every view-set creator (the pitfall in 0028's notes).
- Never invent engine facts: each stage names the file and function it touches at its commit; the RECALLED-VERIFY items in research 0049 section 12 are resolved by reading the code at the start of the stage that needs them.

What the tests pin (the spec's list): a bowl fills from the bottom to the analytic circle (zero contour within one texel of the analytic radius, nested and growing with the supply, the centre's depth equals the level), a plane has no capacity and drains, a tilted plane with one hollow has one basin, a plate with a rim fills evenly and one without has none, two bowls at a saddle (hand-calculated spill heights, Cmax, areas and outlets for both the "lower rim spills first" and the "saddle below both rims" cases), a nested pit is one basin (v1 behaviour), sink texels, the cap, flow direction codes, a hand-calculated catchment (kappa 1.5), pits touching only at a corner are two basins. Properties on seeded random grids (ties are common): the filled height equals a brute-force minimax fixpoint on 40 grids, capacity in [0, cap] and 0 on sinks, filling a filled surface changes nothing and leaves no pit, following `parent` never rises and always ends at a sink or the edge, a texel with a never-rising path to a sink has no capacity, basin bookkeeping is consistent, identical bytes on repeated runs, the eight symmetries of the grid give the symmetric result, and a piece of the grid (its ring a sink) is never higher than the whole and is equal wherever the whole's filled height is below the lowest ground of the ring (the region-independence test, stated in that form because the spec's "halo of one cell" version does not hold in general). A regression pin of one seeded result (this implementation's own output, not an oracle). Bad input (NaN or infinite heights, wrong sizes, zero or negative sizes, bad cap, grids over the caps of side 4096 and 4 Mi texels) is refused with the texel's place, before any allocation.

Deviations from the spec text, on purpose:
- Equal-height ties in the flood are broken by insertion order (first in, first out), not by texel index. The filled height and capacity do not depend on the tie-break at all (tested against the brute-force reference and under all eight symmetries); only the flow directions on flat ground do, and a first-in first-out queue makes a flat drain by the shortest way out (the "epsilon" of the epsilon variant). Insertion order is itself fixed by the scan order, so the result is deterministic.
- `accumulateFlow` is D8 on the flood tree (a texel drains to the neighbour it was reached from, which is its lowest-filled neighbour), not a separate pass on an epsilon-flooded float surface.
- The region independence test is the exact statement above.
- Two known limits of `catchmentRatio`, documented in the header: it counts ground that drains in even from a pit upstream that would first have to fill, and two passes of exactly equal height into one pit can count inflow twice (the ratio is clamped to the grid's size). No test of the second.

Findings for W-b and for toddhow:
1. **Clamping each texel's capacity makes deep hollows flat plates.** Morrowind's terrain has many closed depressions far deeper than a puddle (up to 2,696 units: valleys and the Red Mountain basin). With the spec's per-texel cap of 12, at full supply 12 to 29 percent of the land of two regions would be a 12-unit-deep pond; the same grids measured from the floor (keep the lowest 12 units of each pit) have 0.1 to 0.5 percent. I added `capacityFromFloor` (the shifted capacity, with `min(Cmax, cap)` as the pit's maximum, gives in the run-time formula exactly the depth the uncapped pit has while its level is within the cap; proved by a test over random pits). **W-b should bake the floor-relative form** (fill with an infinite cap, then `capacityFromFloor`); `fillDepressions`' own cap parameter stays for callers that want the spec's literal behaviour. Even then a pit 2,000 units deep shows a 12-unit pond in its bottom whatever the weather; whether a closed valley should hold a puddle at all, and whether very deep pits should be dropped (they are probably drainage the 128-unit grid cannot see), is for toddhow and for the frames of W-b.
2. **The catchment ratio's range [-2, 3] in log2 is far too small for real terrain.** 69 to 88 percent of the basins have a ratio above 8 (mean 118 to 556, a maximum near 99,000): a basin's catchment is the whole slope above it. With the stored range most basins would clip at 8, i.e. fill at Wf x 8. W-b should widen the range (say log2 from 0 to 16 in 8 bits), cap the contributing area at some distance, or both: a format decision (research note section 10).
3. **Most basins are tiny.** The median basin is 2 to 4 texels (noise in the 128-unit grid); the 90th percentile 73 to 276 texels. W-b needs a minimum-area filter (or the pack is mostly specks) and frames to judge it.
4. **The 16-bit height range does not fit the whole of Morrowind.** The land's heights run from -2,152 to 18,952 units (span 21,104); 16 bits at 1/8 unit span 8,191.9. A base per cell (or per region) is needed; the span of one cell was not measured.
5. **The 128-unit vertex grid limits small hollows.** At native spacing the median depression is 2 to 3 texels (256 to 384 units); the 64-unit resample (bilinear, not the engine's triangulation) finds 40 to 50 percent more basins (145 against 103, 504 against 352, 556 against 367), most of them small.
6. A 9 x 9 cell mosaic costs 33 to 37 ms at 128 spacing and 137 to 179 ms at 64 (577 x 577 and 1153 x 1153 texels).

**The finding: Morrowind's paved statics hold no puddles.** The full world, bake settings as the spec has them (spacing 64, cap 12, minimum pond 16 texels, halo 2, radius 1,024): 1,540 cells; 1,276 needed a capture (240 have no reference that can make a surface); **490,157 texels (1.94 percent of the land) in 1,005 cells became static surfaces** (Balmora's cell (-3, -2) 13.4 percent, Vivec's canton cells (3, -11) and (3, -12) 26.5 and 23.2 percent), and **not one pit of the whole world lies mostly on a static surface** (931 pits against W-b's 928, 65,018 wet texels against 68,576: the statics took a few ground pits and made none). With the minimum pond lowered to 4 texels for ALL pits: 2,332 pits, **14 mostly on statics** (4 to 8 texels, 1.6 to 12 units deep; for example cell (-1, 1) at world (-4992, 13696), a rock in the sea, and (2, -12) at (21056, -97920)); the thousands of dips under 4 texels (about 128,000 small ponds counted over the windows) are the noise of collision meshes. The reason is simple: the collision shapes of Morrowind's paving, walkways, bridge decks and platforms are flat, and a flat raised plate drains over its edge. **The Seyda Neen road is not a static**: in the `statics` debug view at the road pose (`runs\dbgrames\screenshot007.png`) the ground under and along it is green (loaded, not static) while buildings and people are dark red, and the cell's 484 static texels (3.0 percent) lie elsewhere (docks, building bases), so statics change nothing on the road (the stage W-d log's "it is a static: stage W-c" was wrong). **The task's goal of a puddle drawn on the Seyda Neen road was therefore not achieved and cannot be by statics**: the road is flat terrain, the pit model gives flat ground no puddle, and only the noise fallback or stage W-e's runtime topographic fallback can.

**What the frames show** (`I:\Projects\OpenEden-work\puddlesc\runs`, never committed; quiet window, `OPENEDEN_FIXED_FRAME_TIME`, seed 1, the weather of every visited region set at the start, noon, steady rain, each place visited twice with the second shot kept, because the simulation runs faster than the map's worker): Balmora's bridge and river walkways from 700 units up (`before2`, `keep`, `dry`, `dbg2`, `wrong_glsl`, `wrong_slang2`, frame 2 of each). (1) **`wetness puddle debug = statics`** (GLSL): the bridge deck and the two river walkways are magenta, exactly their upward faces; walls, roofs and undersides are dark red (not covered), the ground, river bed and plaza green. The capture and the filter see the right things. (2) **Statics-off pack (stage W-d)**: noise puddles lie on the walkway tops and the bridge deck. (3) **Statics pack, `wetness puddle static noise = false`**: the walkways and the bridge deck are **dry** (wet darkening only), as the data says: no pit, no puddle. The ground pits (the plaza on the left) are unchanged. (4) **Statics pack, `static noise = true` (the default)**: the deck and walkways have noise puddles again, as in W-d. Frames of Vivec and Seyda Neen were not usable (the camera landed beside a wall; Seyda Neen's road is terrain); the 14 static pits were not shown (a rock in the sea).

**Determinism and size.** Two bakes of the Seyda Neen cell in fresh folders (fresh captures, fresh pack) are byte-identical (SHA-256 of the 25,312 byte packs equal) and their 24 capture files are identical, so the ray casts are deterministic. The full-world bake with captures present again took 141 s (the digest pass over 1,540 windows included); the first full run, 1,276 captures included, 1,051 s (about 0.7 s a captured cell; the game loads each cell and waits 60 frames). Pack with statics 26,723,600 bytes (W-b, statics off: 25,793,752); the capture folder 87 MB (about 66 KB a cell, kept between bakes, never committed). The run-time staleness check recomputes the references' digest (the content files and the BLAKE3 of every model they name, cached per path) on the worker: the first window of a statics pack opened about 1 to 3 s after the start in these runs (the window at the start cell appeared 1.5 to 2.3 s after the pack line).

**Cost** (RTX 4070, 1280 x 720, `gpu timing`, Balmora's bridge from 700 units up in steady rain, median of the last 800 of about 1,500 frames, three interleaved runs each, `tools/puddlemap_check/scen_cost2.txt`, a shared machine): GPU main scene pass **map off 0.762, 0.764, 0.767 ms; map on with the statics-off pack 0.925, 0.932, 0.945 ms; map on with the statics pack 0.919, 0.923, 0.932 ms**; wall time 3.50 ms in all nine runs (frame-limited, not GPU-limited here). So the object shader reading the map costs about +0.16 to +0.18 ms in this scene (+21 percent of the main pass), whichever pack is loaded: the cost is the object pixels' reads (four texels and four tops through a storage buffer), not the statics. In stage W-d the terrain alone cost nothing measurable; the objects are many more pixels in a town. One scene on one machine is still not a cost note (`docs/research/00NN-puddle-cost.md`, stage W-f, is still to write); a cheaper path is possible (early out on a per-cell "no capacity" bit, or only read the map where the noise mask is above the start threshold).

**Decisions for toddhow (the stage's gate: go, adjust filters or drop statics):** (1) Is a paving that is dry under the map acceptable? The data cannot give Morrowind's paving a puddle, so with statics on and `wetness puddle static noise = false` it loses the noise puddles it had. The default is `true` (keep them on a static surface with no baked pit; the map takes over only where it found a pit), which makes statics on a no-regression setting that adds the 14 pits and shows the height test working; my recommendation. (2) The filter constants (0.5 unit rise, slope 0.5, 2 x 2 width, 16 texels) are first values by reading the data, not tuned by eye; they only matter where a pit exists. (3) A separate, smaller minimum pond for static surfaces (4 texels gives the 14) would be one more baked value; the honest alternative is the micro relief of stage W-e (a procedural dip texture, or the topographic fallback) for flat static surfaces. (4) The capture takes a quarter of an hour for the whole world and needs the game; a mod list changes the references and recaptures only the cells whose references changed.

### Stage W-e (first slice): the topographic fallback (2026-11-11, Windows only, local commit on branch `puddlese`, not pushed)

Resumed after an agent died mid-task: its uncommitted tree was taken over and finished (the build it left running was a retry loop that kept dying out of heap; rebuilt with `-j 6` once memory was free). **Scope: only the terrain fallback.** Ripples and splashes, flow streaks, SSR and interior drips are not built.

**What it does.** `[Rendering] wetness puddle topographic` (off) and `wetness puddle topographic strength` (1, range 0 to 2; needs `wetness puddle map`): where the map covers a terrain pixel and found NO pit there (a road, plateau, field: it used to be dry), the pixel gets small shallow puddles from the fine shape of the ground. The dip of a texel is the mean of its four neighbours' heights (the `top` channel the buffer already holds) minus its own; four texels are blended bilinearly (12 height reads), `dipFactor = smoothstep(0.4, 4, dip)`. A finer noise (64 unit cell; the old puddles use 120) plus 0.35 x `dipFactor` is compared with a threshold that falls with a ramp (the water the ground has taken up beyond what it absorbs, the same supply and absorption as the pits, so the puddles appear when the map's do and shrink as it dries); the largest coverage is 0.3 x strength, times the old flatness of the normal; depth 0.6 units x coverage, ripples at most half grown. Pure twin `Render::PuddleMap::groundDip`, `topoRamp`, `topoCoverage` in `components/render/puddlemapgpu.hpp`; GLSL and Slang twins in `lib/material/puddlemap.*`; the buffer gets word 11 bit 1 (`optionTopographic`) and word 12 (strength bits, **written only when the option is on**, so with it off the buffer is word for word the one before this stage). The terrain fragment stages (both languages) now pass their real normal's Z to `puddleShadeAt` (terrain mode used the constant 1.0 and reads it nowhere else, so off is unchanged). New debug view `wetness puddle debug = relief` (green the hollowness, blue the fallback's puddle, red a map pit, dark red not covered). Objects are NOT touched.
